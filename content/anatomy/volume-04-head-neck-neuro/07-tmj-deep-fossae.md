## Temporomandibular Joint and Mastication

The temporomandibular joint is a synovial joint between the mandibular condyle and mandibular fossa/articular tubercle of temporal bone. It contains an articular disc and permits hinge and gliding movements.

Early mouth opening is mainly hinge movement; wider opening involves anterior translation of the condyle onto the articular tubercle. Muscles of mastication are masseter, temporalis, medial pterygoid, and lateral pterygoid. Motor supply is mandibular division of trigeminal nerve, V3. Lateral pterygoid helps protrude and depress the mandible and acts on the disc/condyle. Auriculotemporal nerve and masseteric/deep temporal branches are important around the joint.

## Infratemporal and Pterygopalatine Fossae

The infratemporal and pterygopalatine fossae are deep face spaces. They are high-yield because they contain V3 branches, maxillary artery branches, venous plexuses, and autonomic ganglia.

Infratemporal fossa contains muscles of mastication, mandibular nerve branches, maxillary artery, pterygoid venous plexus, and chorda tympani joining lingual nerve. Pterygopalatine fossa contains maxillary nerve V2, pterygopalatine ganglion, and terminal maxillary artery branches. Greater petrosal nerve carries preganglionic parasympathetic fibers to pterygopalatine ganglion. Postganglionic fibers from pterygopalatine ganglion reach lacrimal, nasal, palatine, and pharyngeal glands. Foramen rotundum brings V2 into pterygopalatine fossa.

## Why is the pterygopalatine fossa a useful anatomical distribution hub?

It is a small space with several communications to orbit, nasal cavity, palate, pharynx and adjacent deep facial regions. V2, terminal maxillary arterial branches and pterygopalatine ganglion relationships meet there before branches disperse. Postganglionic parasympathetic fibers can travel with sensory branches toward glands, so the carrier branch name does not identify the original autonomic source. A useful map starts with the fossa and then follows each exit to its destination rather than memorizing contents without their onward routes.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The TMJ disc divides the joint into compartments supporting rotation and translation. The condyle moves toward the articular tubercle during wider opening. Mastication is supplied by V3, which enters the infratemporal region through foramen ovale. V2 instead reaches the pterygopalatine fossa through foramen rotundum. These neighboring spaces communicate, but each has its own contents and exits. Link a nerve to its foramen before following its distal branches.
