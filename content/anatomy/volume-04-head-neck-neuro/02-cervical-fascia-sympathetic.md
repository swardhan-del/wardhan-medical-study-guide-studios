## Deep Cervical Fascia and Carotid Sheath

The deep cervical fascia organizes the neck into layers and compartments. The carotid sheath is a vertical fascial tube containing the carotid artery medially, internal jugular vein laterally, and vagus nerve posteriorly between them.

Investing fascia surrounds sternocleidomastoid and trapezius. Pretracheal fascia encloses visceral structures such as thyroid, trachea, and esophagus. Prevertebral fascia surrounds vertebral column and deep neck muscles. The retropharyngeal space can communicate inferiorly with the posterior mediastinum. Carotid sheath contents are carotid artery, internal jugular vein, vagus nerve, plus deep cervical lymphatic relations.

## Cervical Sympathetic Trunk

The cervical sympathetic trunk is the neck portion of the paravertebral sympathetic chain. It contains superior, middle, and inferior cervical ganglia and sends postganglionic fibers to the head and neck mainly through carotid plexuses.

Preganglionic sympathetic neurons for the head arise from upper thoracic spinal cord segments. Fibers ascend and synapse mainly in the superior cervical ganglion. Postganglionic fibers follow internal and external carotid arteries to the eye, glands, vessels, skin, and scalp. Sympathetic pathway to the eye: hypothalamus to ciliospinal center to superior cervical ganglion to internal carotid plexus to long ciliary nerves. Damage may produce Horner syndrome: ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis, and apparent enophthalmos.

## How can a sympathetic lesion affect the eye without damaging an ocular motor nerve?

Descending central pathways connect to upper thoracic sympathetic neurons, whose axons ascend to the superior cervical ganglion. Postganglionic fibers then reach ocular smooth-muscle targets along carotid-associated routes. Interruption can produce miosis and mild ptosis through loss of dilator and superior tarsal muscle activity. This is different from a III lesion affecting skeletal levator function and pupillary constriction pathways. Anhidrosis depends on lesion location because sudomotor fibers diverge along different arterial routes.
