## Basal Ganglia and Internal Capsule

The basal ganglia are deep gray nuclei involved in motor control loops. The internal capsule is the major white matter highway between cortex, thalamus, brainstem, and spinal cord.

Caudate nucleus has head, body, and tail. Lentiform nucleus consists of putamen and globus pallidus. Internal capsule parts: anterior limb, genu, posterior limb, retrolenticular, and sublenticular parts. Anterior limb lies between caudate head and lentiform nucleus. Posterior limb lies between thalamus and lentiform nucleus. Corticospinal fibers pass mainly through posterior limb; corticobulbar fibers pass near genu.

## Thalamus and Hypothalamus

The thalamus is the major sensory and motor relay to cortex, while the hypothalamus is the major autonomic, endocrine, homeostatic, and limbic regulatory center forming the floor and lower lateral walls of the third ventricle.

Thalamus lies lateral to third ventricle and medial to internal capsule. Pulvinar is the posterior thalamic expansion. Lateral geniculate nucleus relays visual information. Medial geniculate nucleus relays auditory information. Hypothalamus relates to optic chiasm, infundibulum, tuber cinereum, and mammillary bodies. Mammillary bodies are limbic structures involved in memory circuitry.

## How do the thalamus and hypothalamus differ despite sharing the diencephalon?

The thalamus participates in major sensory, motor and associative relay circuits, with nuclei specialized for different connections. The hypothalamus coordinates autonomic, endocrine and homeostatic functions through connections including the pituitary and brainstem. Both border the third ventricle but occupy different regions and networks. Lateral and medial geniculate nuclei relay visual and auditory information respectively. Do not identify a function merely from proximity to the ventricle; locate the specific nuclear region and trace its principal connections.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The internal capsule is white matter between deep gray-matter structures. Its anterior limb separates caudate head from lentiform nucleus, and its posterior limb separates thalamus from lentiform nucleus. Corticospinal fibers are concentrated in the posterior limb, while corticobulbar fibers are associated with the genu. Basal ganglia participate in movement selection through loops; they do not send a simple direct motor nerve to skeletal muscle. Thalamic and hypothalamic functions also need to be distinguished.
