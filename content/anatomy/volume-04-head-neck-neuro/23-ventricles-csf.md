## Ventricular System and CSF Flow

The ventricular system contains CSF and consists of paired lateral ventricles, third ventricle, cerebral aqueduct, and fourth ventricle. CSF exits the fourth ventricle into subarachnoid space through median and lateral apertures.

Lateral ventricles communicate with third ventricle through foramina of Monro. Third ventricle communicates with fourth ventricle through cerebral aqueduct. Fourth ventricle opens via median aperture of Magendie and lateral apertures of Luschka. CSF is produced mainly by choroid plexus. CSF drainage includes arachnoid granulations into dural venous pathways and additional lymphatic-associated routes.

## Lateral Ventricles and Internal Brain Landmarks

The lateral ventricles have anterior horn, body, atrium, posterior horn, and inferior horn. They are useful landmarks for caudate nucleus, corpus callosum, fornix, thalamus, hippocampus, and internal capsule.

Anterior horn lies in frontal lobe; body lies under corpus callosum. Atrium is the junction region of body, posterior horn, and inferior horn. Posterior horn extends into occipital lobe. Inferior horn extends into temporal lobe and relates to hippocampal formation. Caudate nucleus follows the C-shaped contour of lateral ventricle. Septum pellucidum separates anterior horns.

## Why do ventricular enlargement patterns help identify the site of obstruction?

A blockage interrupts a connected fluid route, so cavities upstream can enlarge differently from those downstream. Aqueduct obstruction separates lateral and third ventricles from the fourth-ventricular outlet route. Obstruction farther downstream can affect a broader ventricular pattern. This is a topological reasoning exercise, not a diagnosis based only on ventricle size. CSF production, drainage, tissue compliance and chronicity also influence appearance, so the map provides one necessary part of interpretation rather than the whole explanation.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

It reaches subarachnoid spaces through median and lateral apertures. Obstruction at the aqueduct can enlarge upstream ventricles while sparing the fourth from that direct upstream dilation pattern.
