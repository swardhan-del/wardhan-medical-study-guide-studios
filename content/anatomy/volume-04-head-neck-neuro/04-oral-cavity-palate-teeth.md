## Oral Cavity Overview

The oral cavity is divided into the vestibule and oral cavity proper. The vestibule lies between lips/cheeks and dental arches. The oral cavity proper lies inside the dental arches and extends posteriorly to the oropharynx through the isthmus of the fauces.

Roof: hard and soft palate. Floor: mylohyoid, geniohyoid, tongue, and mucosa of the oral floor. Lateral/anterior boundaries: teeth, gingivae, alveolar arches, lips, and cheeks. Posterior opening: isthmus of the fauces, bounded by soft palate, palatoglossal arches, and tongue. Parotid duct opens into the vestibule opposite the upper second molar.

## Palate

The palate separates oral and nasal cavities. The hard palate is bony anteriorly; the soft palate is muscular posteriorly and participates in swallowing, speech, and closure of the nasopharynx.

Hard palate is formed mainly by palatine processes of maxilla and horizontal plates of palatine bones. Soft palate contains palatine aponeurosis and muscles: tensor veli palatini, levator veli palatini, musculus uvulae, palatoglossus, and palatopharyngeus. Tensor veli palatini is supplied by V3; most other soft palate muscles are supplied by vagus via pharyngeal plexus. Sensory supply of hard palate is mainly greater palatine and nasopalatine nerves from V2. Cleft palate results from failure of palatal fusion during development.

## Teeth

Each tooth has crown, neck, and root. Hard tissues include enamel, dentin, and cementum; soft tissue is dental pulp. Teeth are anchored in alveoli by the periodontal ligament.

Anatomical crown is covered by enamel; clinical crown is the part visible in the oral cavity. Permanent dentition has 32 teeth: per quadrant, 2 incisors, 1 canine, 2 premolars, 3 molars. Deciduous dentition has 20 teeth: per quadrant, 2 incisors, 1 canine, 2 molars. Upper molar roots may be close to the maxillary sinus floor. Inferior alveolar nerve supplies mandibular teeth; superior alveolar branches supply maxillary teeth.

## How do the floor muscles support both oral and swallowing functions?

Mylohyoid forms a muscular sling beneath the oral floor, with geniohyoid and tongue structures above it. Depending on fixation, suprahyoid contraction can elevate the hyoid or help depress the mandible. The submandibular duct and lingual nerve have close relationships in this region, while the hypoglossal nerve supplies tongue motor function. A floor-of-mouth diagram should distinguish muscle, duct and sensory nerve rather than treating every cord-like structure as the same kind of pathway.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The vestibule lies between lips or cheeks and teeth, whereas the oral cavity proper lies inside the dental arches. This distinction localizes duct openings: parotid opens into the vestibule, submandibular into the floor of the cavity proper. Hard palate separates oral and nasal cavities; soft palate moves during swallowing. Maxillary teeth receive V2 branches and mandibular teeth V3 branches. Motor supply to palate is chiefly vagal, with tensor veli palatini supplied by V3.
