## CN V Trigeminal Nerve

The trigeminal nerve is the major sensory nerve of the face and motor nerve to muscles of mastication. It has ophthalmic V1, maxillary V2, and mandibular V3 divisions.

## Relationships and functional organization

V1 exits superior orbital fissure and carries sensation from forehead, cornea, upper eyelid, and dorsum of nose. V2 exits foramen rotundum and supplies midface, maxillary teeth, palate, and nasal region. V3 exits foramen ovale and is mixed; it supplies lower face sensation and motor to mastication muscles. Motor nucleus supplies mastication, mylohyoid, anterior digastric, tensor tympani, and tensor veli palatini. Trigeminal sensory nuclei include mesencephalic, principal sensory, and spinal trigeminal nuclei.

## How can the corneal reflex separate sensory from motor dysfunction?

Touch-related input from the cornea travels through V1, while eyelid closure uses VII to orbicularis oculi through brainstem connections. If afferent input is interrupted, stimulation of that cornea may fail to produce the expected bilateral response. If one facial motor output is interrupted, that eyelid fails to close regardless of which cornea supplies the input. This illustrates a general localization method: identify input, central connection and output separately instead of attributing an entire reflex to one nerve.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

V1 and V2 are sensory divisions; V3 carries both sensory and branchial motor fibers. The trigeminal ganglion contains most primary sensory neuron cell bodies, but proprioceptive neurons associated with the mesencephalic nucleus are a notable exception. For the corneal reflex, V1 is the afferent limb and VII closes the eyelids. A facial sensory deficit and facial muscle weakness therefore point to different functional pathways even when both involve the face.
