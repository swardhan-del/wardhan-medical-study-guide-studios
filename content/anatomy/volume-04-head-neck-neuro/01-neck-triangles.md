## Regions and Neck Triangles

The neck is a topographic passageway between the head, thorax, and upper limb. The sternocleidomastoid muscle divides each side into anterior and posterior triangles, which organize vessels, nerves, muscles, lymph nodes, and visceral structures.

Anterior triangle lies between midline, mandible, and anterior border of sternocleidomastoid. Posterior triangle lies between posterior border of sternocleidomastoid, trapezius, and clavicle. The hyoid bone is a practical landmark separating suprahyoid and infrahyoid regions. The carotid triangle is high yield because the carotid bifurcation, internal jugular vein, vagus nerve, hypoglossal nerve, and ansa cervicalis are encountered there.

## Cervical Plexus, Ansa Cervicalis, and Phrenic Nerve

The cervical plexus is formed by anterior rami C1-C4 and provides cutaneous branches to the neck plus motor branches to several infrahyoid and prevertebral muscles. The phrenic nerve arises mainly from C3-C5 and descends to the diaphragm.

Cutaneous branches emerge near the posterior border of sternocleidomastoid. Ansa cervicalis supplies most infrahyoid muscles: sternohyoid, sternothyroid, and omohyoid. Thyrohyoid and geniohyoid receive C1 fibers traveling with hypoglossal nerve. Phrenic nerve descends on anterior scalene and passes into the thorax.

## Why does the accessory nerve need special attention in the posterior triangle?

After supplying sternocleidomastoid, the spinal accessory nerve crosses the posterior triangle relatively superficially toward trapezius. Weakness of trapezius can impair scapular elevation and upward rotation, even though the nerve passes through a neck region. This links a local neck course to shoulder function. By contrast, nearby cervical plexus cutaneous branches serve skin and do not replace accessory motor supply. A triangle map should therefore include both the route and the distant muscle action that makes the route functionally important.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

Surface triangles organize access to deeper structures; they are not walled compartments. The anterior triangle is subdivided by digastric and omohyoid muscles, while the inferior belly of omohyoid divides the posterior triangle. The accessory nerve crosses the posterior triangle toward trapezius. Cutaneous cervical plexus branches emerge near the posterior border of sternocleidomastoid, whereas the phrenic nerve descends on anterior scalene deeper in the neck. Learn depth as well as surface outline.
