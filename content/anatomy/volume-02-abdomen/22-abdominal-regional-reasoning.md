## Lesser Sac and Epiploic Foramen

The lesser sac is the omental bursa behind the stomach and lesser omentum. It communicates with the greater sac through the epiploic foramen, also called the omental foramen.

Anterior wall of the lesser sac includes stomach, lesser omentum, and gastrocolic ligament. Posterior wall relates to pancreas, left kidney, left suprarenal gland, aorta, and related retroperitoneal structures. Epiploic foramen boundaries: anterior hepatoduodenal ligament, posterior IVC, superior caudate lobe, inferior first part of duodenum.

## Duodenum

The duodenum is the first part of the small intestine and forms a C-shaped curve around the head of the pancreas. It has superior, descending, horizontal, and ascending parts. Most of it is secondarily retroperitoneal except the proximal first part.

First part is partly intraperitoneal and is related posteriorly to bile duct, portal vein, and IVC. Second part receives bile duct and main pancreatic duct at the major duodenal papilla; accessory pancreatic duct may open at the minor papilla. Third part crosses anterior to IVC and aorta and posterior to superior mesenteric vessels. Fourth part ascends to the duodenojejunal flexure, supported by the suspensory muscle/ligament of duodenum.

## Pancreas

The pancreas is a mostly secondarily retroperitoneal foregut organ behind the stomach and related to the C-loop of duodenum and splenic vessels. It has head, uncinate process, neck, body, and tail.

Head lies in the duodenal curve. Uncinate process extends posterior to superior mesenteric vessels. Neck lies anterior to formation of portal vein. Body crosses toward the spleen; splenic artery runs along superior border and splenic vein posterior to the pancreas. Tail reaches the splenic hilum in the splenorenal ligament region.

## Portal Vein and Caval Drainage

The portal vein carries nutrient-rich venous blood from the gastrointestinal tract, spleen, pancreas, and gallbladder to the liver. It is formed behind the neck of the pancreas by the union of superior mesenteric and splenic veins.

Inferior mesenteric vein usually drains into splenic vein. Portal vein enters the hepatoduodenal ligament and lies posterior to hepatic artery proper and bile duct. Hepatic veins drain blood from liver into the inferior vena cava. Caval structures such as kidneys, suprarenal glands, gonads, posterior abdominal wall, and lower limbs drain directly or indirectly to IVC, not portal vein.
