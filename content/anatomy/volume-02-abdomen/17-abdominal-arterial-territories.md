## Celiac Trunk

The celiac trunk is the unpaired foregut artery from the abdominal aorta near T12/L1. Its classic branches are left gastric, splenic, and common hepatic arteries.

Left gastric supplies abdominal esophagus and lesser curvature region. Splenic artery supplies spleen, pancreas, fundus, and greater curvature through branches. Name its branches clearly: pancreatic branches, short gastric arteries to the fundus, left gastro-omental artery along the greater curvature, and terminal splenic branches to the spleen. Common hepatic gives gastroduodenal artery and continues toward proper hepatic artery. The celiac territory includes abdominal foregut organs and the spleen; the spleen is mesodermal rather than a gut endoderm derivative.

## Superior Mesenteric Artery

The superior mesenteric artery supplies the midgut from distal duodenum to approximately proximal two-thirds of transverse colon. It arises from the abdominal aorta and passes anterior to the third part of the duodenum.

Branches include inferior pancreaticoduodenal, jejunal and ileal arteries, ileocolic, right colic, and middle colic arteries. It supplies distal duodenum, jejunum, ileum, cecum, appendix, ascending colon, and proximal transverse colon. Superior mesenteric vein joins splenic vein behind the neck of pancreas to form portal vein.

## Inferior Mesenteric Artery

The inferior mesenteric artery supplies the hindgut from distal one-third of transverse colon to upper anal canal. Its branches include left colic, sigmoid, and superior rectal arteries.

Left colic supplies descending colon and participates in marginal artery. Sigmoid arteries supply sigmoid colon. The superior rectal artery supplies the rectum. The superior rectal vein, a separate vessel, participates in portal-systemic venous connections. The marginal artery connects colic branches along the colon.

## Why are watershed regions vulnerable despite arterial anastomoses?

An anastomosis provides a possible alternative route, but its capacity depends on caliber, pressure and individual anatomy. Border territories between major arterial supplies may receive less effective flow during generalized low perfusion or proximal obstruction. The splenic flexure is a classic meeting region of superior and inferior mesenteric territories; the rectosigmoid region is another commonly discussed boundary. The existence of a marginal artery therefore does not guarantee protection. Describe the dominant sources, connecting channel and physiological limitation separately.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The three unpaired arteries organize gut territories, but anastomoses bridge their borders. Pancreaticoduodenal arcades connect celiac and superior mesenteric supply; the marginal artery links colic branches. At the splenic flexure, superior and inferior mesenteric territories meet, creating a potential watershed when perfusion is poor. Do not confuse an arterial connection with a portal-systemic venous connection. Both involve vessels, but the pressures, directions and clinical implications differ.
