## Posterior Abdominal Wall

The posterior abdominal wall is formed by lumbar vertebrae, psoas major, iliacus, quadratus lumborum, diaphragm crura, transversalis fascia, nerves, vessels, kidneys, ureters, and retroperitoneal structures.

## Relationships and functional organization

Psoas major lies beside lumbar vertebral bodies and passes under the inguinal ligament with iliacus to form iliopsoas. Quadratus lumborum extends from iliac crest to 12th rib and lumbar transverse processes. Major nerves include subcostal, iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal, genitofemoral, lateral femoral cutaneous, femoral, and obturator nerves. The abdominal aorta and inferior vena cava lie anterior to the vertebral column, with the aorta left of midline and IVC right of midline.

## Why is psoas a useful landmark for both musculoskeletal and visceral anatomy?

Psoas connects lumbar vertebral structures with the femur through the iliopsoas tendon, linking spine and hip mechanically. The lumbar plexus forms within it, and nerves emerge around its borders. Ureter and retroperitoneal vessels lie nearby but remain distinct structures with separate functions. In a sectional image, finding psoas on both sides of the vertebral bodies gives a stable reference for locating kidneys, ureters and major vessels. Muscle position organizes a region without making every neighboring structure part of that muscle.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The lumbar plexus forms within the posterior part of psoas major. Its branches emerge on different sides: the femoral nerve laterally, obturator nerve medially, and genitofemoral nerve anteriorly. This is a three-dimensional map rather than a list of roots. The ureter descends on the psoas region but is a tube, not a plexus branch.
