## Duodenum

The duodenum is the first part of the small intestine and forms a C-shaped curve around the head of the pancreas. It has superior, descending, horizontal, and ascending parts. Most of it is secondarily retroperitoneal except the proximal first part.

## Relationships and functional organization

First part is partly intraperitoneal and is related posteriorly to bile duct, portal vein, and IVC. Second part receives bile duct and main pancreatic duct at the major duodenal papilla; accessory pancreatic duct may open at the minor papilla. Third part crosses anterior to IVC and aorta and posterior to superior mesenteric vessels. Fourth part ascends to the duodenojejunal flexure, supported by the suspensory muscle/ligament of duodenum.

## Why does the duodenum receive both celiac and superior mesenteric arterial contributions?

The foregut-midgut boundary lies in the duodenal region near the biliary entry, so proximal and distal territories receive different primary arterial sources. Superior pancreaticoduodenal branches arise through the gastroduodenal route, while inferior pancreaticoduodenal branches come from the superior mesenteric artery. Their arcades connect the territories around the pancreatic head. This is an arterial anastomosis, not a portal-systemic venous channel. Developmental territory and adult collateral connections should both appear in a complete account of its supply.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The descending part receives the main biliary and pancreatic drainage. The horizontal third part crosses the midline anterior to the aorta and inferior vena cava but posterior to the superior mesenteric vessels. This creates an anatomical compression corridor between the aorta and superior mesenteric artery. The fourth part ascends toward the duodenojejunal flexure. Identifying the part is essential: a relation of one segment should not be transferred to the entire C-loop.
