## Rectum and Anal Canal

The rectum continues from sigmoid colon and follows the sacral curve. The anal canal passes through the pelvic floor and has important arterial, venous, lymphatic, epithelial, and nerve distinctions above and below the pectinate line.

Upper rectum is related to peritoneum anteriorly and laterally; lower rectum becomes infraperitoneal. Superior rectal artery is from IMA; middle rectal from internal iliac; inferior rectal from internal pudendal. Above pectinate line: visceral innervation, portal venous drainage through superior rectal vein, lymph toward internal iliac/inferior mesenteric routes. Below pectinate line: somatic innervation via inferior rectal nerves, systemic venous drainage, superficial inguinal lymph drainage.

## Hindgut, Cloaca, and Anal Canal Development

The hindgut forms distal transverse colon, descending colon, sigmoid colon, rectum, and upper anal canal. The cloaca is partitioned by the urorectal septum into anorectal canal posteriorly and urogenital sinus anteriorly.

Upper anal canal derives from endodermal hindgut. Lower anal canal derives from ectodermal proctodeum. Pectinate line marks the transition between these developmental territories. Developmental origin explains differences in innervation, blood supply, venous drainage, lymphatics, and epithelium.

## How do the two anal sphincters differ in tissue and control?

The internal anal sphincter is a thickening of smooth muscle and contributes involuntary resting tone. The external anal sphincter is skeletal muscle with somatic innervation and contributes voluntary control. Puborectalis is a separate sling affecting the anorectal angle. Continence depends on coordinated sphincters, pelvic support, rectal sensation and stool properties, so no single ring explains it fully. The pectinate line is a mucosal developmental landmark within the canal and is not identical to either sphincter's full extent.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The pectinate line marks a developmental and neurovascular transition within the anal canal. Above it, visceral afferents predominate; below it, somatic afferents convey well-localized pain. Venous and lymphatic territories overlap rather than forming impermeable borders. The rectum has no discrete teniae coli or haustra. Distinguish a mucosal landmark from the anorectal junction at the pelvic floor: they are at different levels and answer different anatomical questions.
