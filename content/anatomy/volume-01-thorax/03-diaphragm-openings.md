# 03. Diaphragm structure and sensation

Volume 1 · 10 minutes · Updated 2026-09-10

The diaphragm combines a central tendon with peripheral muscle attached to sternum, ribs and lumbar structures.

## Locate the structures

The diaphragm separates thoracic and abdominal cavities while allowing vessels, nerves and the esophagus to cross. Sternal, costal and lumbar muscular portions converge on a central tendon. Lumbar attachments include crura and arcuate ligaments. The right dome is usually higher, in part because of the liver beneath it.

## Connect structure and function

Phrenic nerves from C3-C5 provide motor supply. Sensory innervation differs by region: central diaphragmatic pleura and peritoneum use phrenic afferents, while peripheral regions use lower intercostal afferents. Referred pain can therefore be perceived away from the irritated tissue. Motor paralysis and sensory referral are different consequences of involvement of the same regional nerve system.

## Why does the esophageal hiatus have a different mechanical relationship from the aortic route?

The esophageal opening is formed largely within muscular crural tissue, so diaphragmatic activity contributes to the functional anti-reflux region around the esophagus. The aorta passes behind the diaphragm under an arcuate ligament rather than through the contractile central muscle in the same way. The caval opening lies in tendon and changes with diaphragmatic movement differently again. The T8-T10-T12 mnemonic is useful only when paired with these material and positional differences.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The diaphragm combines a central tendon with peripheral muscle attached to sternum, ribs and lumbar structures. Contraction lowers the domes and expands the thorax. The main opening levels are conventionally T8 for cava, T10 for esophagus and T12 for aorta, but their construction differs: the caval opening is tendinous, the esophageal hiatus muscular, and the aorta passes posteriorly beneath the median arcuate ligament. Memorize structure and relationship with the level.
