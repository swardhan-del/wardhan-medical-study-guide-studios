# 10. Posterior mediastinal routes

Volume 1 · 10 minutes · Updated 2026-09-10

The posterior mediastinum contains the esophagus, descending aorta, thoracic duct and azygos pathways among other structures.

## Locate the structures

The esophagus descends posterior to the trachea and then behind the left atrium before crossing the diaphragm. Its course has characteristic constrictions associated with the pharyngoesophageal junction, aortic and bronchial crossings, and diaphragmatic hiatus. A relation at one height should not be assumed to persist along the entire tube.

## Connect structure and function

The azygos vein ascends on the right and arches over the right lung root to enter SVC. Hemiazygos and accessory hemiazygos pathways drain portions of the left posterior thorax toward it. The thoracic duct carries lymph rather than venous blood, usually entering through the aortic hiatus and ascending toward the left venous angle. Its relationship to the esophagus, aorta and azygos vein helps distinguish these parallel structures.

## Why is the azygos system a collateral venous route rather than a second portal system?

It connects systemic venous territories along the thoracic and abdominal walls and ultimately drains toward SVC. Portal circulation instead links a first capillary bed to liver sinusoids before systemic return. The azygos route can communicate with other systemic channels when major flow paths are altered, but it does not normally insert a second organ capillary bed in that manner. Distinguish the azygos vein from the adjacent thoracic duct, which carries lymph to a venous junction.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The posterior mediastinum contains the esophagus, descending aorta, thoracic duct and azygos pathways among other structures. The thoracic duct ascends from the abdomen, usually crosses toward the left in the thorax and reaches the junction of left internal jugular and subclavian veins. It drains most of the body except the right upper quadrant. Azygos veins provide systemic venous connections and enter the SVC; they are not lymphatic channels. Both networks vary, so recognize a typical map rather than a mandatory single tube pattern.
