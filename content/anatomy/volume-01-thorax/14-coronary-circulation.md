## Locate the structures

Right and left coronary arteries arise from their corresponding aortic sinuses. The left main artery usually divides into anterior interventricular and circumflex branches. RCA follows the right atrioventricular groove and gives branches including right marginal and commonly nodal and posterior interventricular branches. Individual branching and supply territories vary.

## Connect structure and function

Great cardiac vein accompanies the anterior interventricular region, middle cardiac vein the posterior interventricular region, and small cardiac vein portions of the right coronary route. Much venous return joins the coronary sinus in the posterior atrioventricular groove before entering right atrium. Anterior cardiac veins can enter the right atrium directly, while small intramural veins open into chambers.

## Why is left ventricular coronary perfusion especially related to diastole?

During systole, contracting left ventricular myocardium compresses intramural vessels, limiting flow particularly in deeper layers. During diastole the muscle relaxes while aortic pressure continues to drive coronary inflow. This links coronary anatomy to the cardiac cycle without making all coronary flow exclusively diastolic or identical in both ventricles. Epicardial arterial position, intramural compression and aortic driving pressure are separate parts of the explanation. Dominance describes branching origin, not the phase in which flow occurs.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The coronary arteries arise from the aortic sinuses and distribute across the epicardial surface before penetrating myocardium. LAD supplies important anterior ventricular and septal territories; circumflex follows the left atrioventricular groove. Dominance concerns posterior interventricular origin and varies between people. Coronary sinus drainage reaches the right atrium, but anterior cardiac and smallest cardiac veins provide additional routes. Territory maps are typical patterns, not guarantees that one named blockage produces identical damage in every heart.
