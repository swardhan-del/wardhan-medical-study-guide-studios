## Locate the structures

The thoracic cage consists of twelve pairs of ribs and their cartilages, thoracic vertebrae and sternum. Typical ribs have a head, neck, tubercle, shaft, angle and costal groove. The head and tubercle articulate with vertebral structures, allowing small movements that together change chest dimensions.

## Connect structure and function

Elevation of ribs changes anteroposterior and transverse dimensions through combined pump-handle and bucket-handle patterns. Costal cartilage adds compliance. The first rib is broad and has vascular grooves separated by the scalene tubercle; ribs 11 and 12 lack the typical anterior and tubercular articulations. Bone identification begins with these distinguishing features, not with counting alone.

## Why are pump-handle and bucket-handle movements useful approximations rather than separate mechanisms?

Each rib rotates through its vertebral articulations with a three-dimensional axis. The visible result includes changes in both anteroposterior and transverse chest dimensions, with different emphases at different levels. Pump-handle and bucket-handle descriptions isolate components to make the movement understandable. Cartilage compliance and sternum movement also contribute. A rib is therefore not a simple lever restricted to one plane, and normal respiration combines rib motion with diaphragm descent and changes in abdominal pressure.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The second costal cartilage articulates at the sternal angle, so it is the landmark from which the other ribs can be counted. The sternal angle is the manubriosternal junction and provides a practical starting point for rib counting. Its transverse plane is classically near the T4-T5 disc and separates superior from inferior mediastinum. These are approximate living relationships that change with posture and respiration. A palpable landmark helps orient deeper anatomy but is not an exact ruler for every person. Ribs 11 and 12 lack anterior attachment; ribs 8-10 attach indirectly through the costal margin.
