## Locate the structures

Each intercostal space contains external, internal and innermost muscle layers with intervening neurovascular structures. External fibers generally run inferoanteriorly, while internal fibers run in a different direction. Membranous portions replace muscle near parts of the anterior or posterior ends, so a cross-section depends on where it is taken.

## Connect structure and function

Posterior intercostal arteries arise mainly from the thoracic aorta, with the highest spaces supplied through the supreme intercostal route. Anterior branches arise from internal thoracic and musculophrenic arteries in the upper spaces. These arteries communicate within the wall. The main neurovascular bundle occupies the costal groove; lateral and anterior cutaneous nerve branches extend toward skin.

## Why do intercostal arteries have both anterior and posterior sources?

The thoracic wall receives branches approaching from the aortic side posteriorly and the internal thoracic system anteriorly. Their communications supply the elongated intercostal space and create collateral routes. The highest and lowest spaces differ from the standard middle-space pattern, so one drawing should be labeled as typical rather than universal. Nerves and vessels share a plane but have different origins and directions. Trace arterial inflow separately from venous return and segmental nerve distribution.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The main bundle lies near the inferior border of the rib above, with vein, artery and nerve ordered superior to inferior. Collateral branches also exist, so the main-bundle mnemonic does not define a risk-free procedural path. Intercostal nerves are anterior rami of T1-T11; the T12 anterior ramus is subcostal. Lower intercostal nerves continue into the abdominal wall. Relate the nerve's segmental origin to both its muscular and cutaneous targets.
