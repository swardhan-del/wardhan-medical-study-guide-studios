# 08. Mediastinal compartments and vessels

Volume 1 · 10 minutes · Updated 2026-09-10

## Locate the structures

The superior mediastinum contains the aortic arch and major branches, brachiocephalic veins and upper SVC, trachea, esophagus, thymic tissue and traversing nerves and lymphatics. The inferior mediastinum is divided into a small anterior region, a middle region around pericardium and a posterior region behind it.

## Connect structure and function

The ascending aorta leaves the left ventricle, becomes the arch and continues as descending thoracic aorta. The brachiocephalic trunk divides into right common carotid and right subclavian arteries; the corresponding left arteries usually arise separately. Venous return is more symmetrical in its proximal formation: each brachiocephalic vein forms from internal jugular and subclavian veins, then both unite into SVC.

## Why should the sternal-angle plane not be treated as an exact fixed level for every structure?

Living structures move with respiration, posture and changes in organ volume. The T4-T5 association is a conventional reference that organizes the mediastinum and several important landmarks, including the tracheal bifurcation and aortic arch region. Individual measurements vary. The plane is most useful as a relational guide, not as proof that every listed structure occupies one mathematical slice. State the approximate level and use neighboring structures to confirm orientation.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The mediastinum lies between the pleural sacs and extends from inlet to diaphragm. The sternal-angle plane separates superior from inferior parts; pericardium then helps divide the inferior part into anterior, middle and posterior regions. The aortic arch gives brachiocephalic, left common carotid and left subclavian branches in the usual pattern. Brachiocephalic veins instead form by internal jugular-subclavian union and join as the SVC. Similar names do not imply symmetrical arterial and venous branching.
