# 12. Chambers and internal landmarks

## Locate the structures

Systemic venous blood enters right atrium and passes through tricuspid valve into right ventricle. The right ventricle sends it through pulmonary valve toward lungs. Pulmonary veins return blood to left atrium, which empties through mitral valve into left ventricle and then through aortic valve. This flow sequence is independent of the observer's viewing angle.

## Connect structure and function

The right atrium shows the fossa ovalis and crista terminalis. Ventricles contain trabeculae carneae and papillary muscles; the right ventricular outflow becomes the smooth conus arteriosus. The left ventricular wall is thicker because it generates systemic pressures, not because it necessarily ejects more blood each beat. The interventricular septum has a large muscular and a small membranous component.

## Why does the left ventricle have a thicker wall without normally pumping more volume than the right?

The two ventricles operate in series, so their average outputs must remain balanced over time. The left ventricle generates higher pressure to drive systemic circulation, whereas the right supplies the lower-resistance pulmonary circuit. Wall thickness reflects the differing mechanical demands rather than a permanently greater stroke volume on the left. External chamber size, internal cavity volume and muscle mass are related but distinct measurements, and should not be used interchangeably on a heart diagram.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The heart is oblique, so chamber names do not translate directly into front-back positions. The right ventricle is mainly anterior, the left atrium contributes most of the posterior base, and the left ventricle forms the apex. The esophagus lies behind the left atrium. Internally, atrial pectinate muscles differ from ventricular trabeculae carneae; the right atrium has a crista terminalis separating smooth and rough regions. Use external position and internal texture together to identify a chamber.
