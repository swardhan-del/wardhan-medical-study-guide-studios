## Cubital Fossa and Forearm Compartments

The cubital fossa is the anterior elbow triangle. Its deep contents are biceps tendon, brachial artery, median nerve, and radial nerve laterally; the median cubital vein is superficial.

## Relationships and functional organization

Borders: superior interepicondylar line, medial pronator teres, lateral brachioradialis. Floor: brachialis and supinator. Roof: cubital fascia and bicipital aponeurosis. Anterior forearm is mainly flexor-pronator and mostly median nerve. FCU and medial half of FDP are ulnar nerve. Posterior forearm is extensor-supinator and radial/deep radial/posterior interosseous nerve.

## Why is pronation-supination not simply rotation of the hand at the wrist?

The radius rotates relative to ulna at proximal and distal radioulnar joints, carrying the hand with it. In pronation the radius crosses the ulna; in supination they are approximately parallel. The wrist articulations then position the hand on this rotating framework. Supinator and biceps contribute to supination, while pronator teres and quadratus contribute to pronation. A drawing that puts all rotation at the radiocarpal joint would miss the long-bone mechanics and the two radioulnar articulations.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

Most anterior forearm muscles receive median supply, but flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial part of flexor digitorum profundus are ulnar exceptions. The posterior compartment uses radial-derived branches. In the cubital fossa, biceps tendon, brachial artery and median nerve lie in a lateral-to-medial sequence, with the radial nerve farther laterally. The bicipital aponeurosis separates deeper neurovascular structures from superficial veins. Surface and deep structures must be placed in separate layers.
