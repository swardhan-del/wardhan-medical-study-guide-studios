## Glenohumeral Joint and Rotator Cuff

The shoulder joint is a multiaxial ball-and-socket joint between the humeral head and glenoid cavity. It is highly mobile but relatively unstable. Stability depends on the glenoid labrum, capsule, ligaments, and especially the rotator cuff.

Surfaces: humeral head and glenoid cavity. Capsule attaches to glenoid margin and anatomical neck of humerus. Labrum deepens the glenoid socket. Rotator cuff muscles: supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis. Supraspinatus initiates abduction. Subscapularis medially rotates; infraspinatus and teres minor laterally rotate.

## Shoulder Girdle Muscles

Shoulder girdle muscles move the scapula and humerus. For oral exams, memorize each muscle by origin-region, insertion, nerve, and action rather than isolated lists.

Trapezius moves scapula and is supplied by accessory nerve. Latissimus dorsi extends, adducts, and medially rotates humerus; supplied by thoracodorsal nerve. Serratus anterior protracts and rotates scapula; supplied by long thoracic nerve. Rhomboids retract scapula; supplied by dorsal scapular nerve. Deltoid abducts the arm after initiation by supraspinatus; supplied by axillary nerve.

## How does the cuff stabilize a shallow glenoid while permitting mobility?

The glenoid is relatively shallow compared with the humeral head, so muscles and capsule provide substantial dynamic support. Cuff forces compress and center the head while larger movers generate torque. The labrum deepens the rim and increases effective contact, but it does not turn the shoulder into a deeply constrained hip-like socket. Capsular support is not uniform around the joint. Understanding this balance explains how a highly mobile joint can be stable during coordinated movement yet vulnerable when forces or supports change.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The cuff consists of supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor and subscapularis. Their tendons blend with the capsule and help center the humeral head during movement. Deltoid contributes powerful elevation, while serratus anterior and trapezius rotate the scapula. Traditional angular rules about which muscle starts or continues abduction are teaching approximations; muscles overlap in action. Teres major shares a regional name with teres minor but is not a cuff muscle and has a different action and nerve supply.
