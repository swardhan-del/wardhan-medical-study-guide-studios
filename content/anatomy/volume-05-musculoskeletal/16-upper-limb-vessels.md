## Upper Limb Arteries, Veins, and Lymphatics

Upper limb arteries run subclavian to axillary to brachial to radial and ulnar arteries. Superficial veins are cephalic laterally, basilic medially, and median cubital between them. Upper limb lymph mainly drains to axillary nodes.

Radial artery crosses the anatomical snuffbox and contributes mainly to the deep palmar arch. Ulnar artery contributes mainly to the superficial palmar arch. Cephalic vein travels in the deltopectoral groove and drains to axillary vein. Basilic vein pierces brachial fascia and joins brachial veins. Median cubital vein is a common venipuncture site. Upper limb lymph ultimately reaches axillary nodes.

## Axillary Nodes and Upper Limb Drainage

Axillary nodes are arranged into pectoral, subscapular, humeral, central, and apical groups. Most upper limb lymph drains through humeral nodes to central nodes, apical nodes, and the subclavian lymph trunk.

Pectoral/anterior nodes drain anterior thoracic wall and lateral breast. Subscapular/posterior nodes drain posterior thoracic wall and scapular region. Humeral/lateral nodes drain most upper limb. Central nodes receive from pectoral, subscapular, and humeral nodes. Apical nodes receive from central nodes and drain to subclavian lymph trunk. Cephalic pathway may pass to deltopectoral/infraclavicular nodes then apical nodes.

## Why are the superficial and deep palmar arches useful connections rather than perfect duplicates?

The superficial arch is usually predominantly ulnar and the deep arch predominantly radial, with communicating contributions that vary. They distribute blood to different branches and levels of the palm while providing potential collateral routes. An incomplete arch or altered vessel caliber changes that capacity. Venous return and lymphatic drainage use different networks, so an arterial arch drawing should not be reused unchanged for them. Identify the dominant source, connecting contribution and distal branches for each arch.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The arterial route changes names at landmarks: subclavian becomes axillary at the lateral first rib, then brachial at the inferior border of teres major. Radial and ulnar branches contribute to palmar arches, with variable completeness. Superficial veins follow different paths from deep arteries: cephalic runs laterally and basilic medially. Most lymph reaches axillary groups, but the cephalic-associated route may reach deltopectoral or apical nodes. Do not assume one rigid drainage pattern for every person.
