## Neck Muscles: Superficial, Suprahyoid, Infrahyoid

Neck muscle questions are easiest if grouped by layer and nerve. Sternocleidomastoid and trapezius are supplied by the accessory nerve. Suprahyoids elevate hyoid/floor of mouth; infrahyoids depress/stabilize hyoid and larynx.

## Relationships and functional organization

Platysma is facial nerve. Sternocleidomastoid: accessory nerve; contralateral rotation and ipsilateral lateral flexion. Trapezius: accessory nerve; moves scapula. Digastric anterior belly and mylohyoid: mylohyoid nerve V3. Digastric posterior belly and stylohyoid: facial nerve. Geniohyoid and thyrohyoid: C1 via hypoglossal. Most infrahyoids: ansa cervicalis.

## How can suprahyoid contraction have opposite-looking effects in different tasks?

If the mandible is stabilized, suprahyoid muscles can elevate the hyoid and help move the laryngeal complex during swallowing. If the hyoid is stabilized by other muscles, some of the same muscles help depress the mandible. The effect depends on which attachment is fixed and the coordinated activity of neighboring groups. Infrahyoids therefore provide a functional reference point rather than simply acting as antagonists in every situation. Include both attachments when explaining action, and keep their mixed nerve sources distinct.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion
