## Vagina

The vagina is a fibromuscular canal from vestibule to cervix, forming anterior, posterior, and lateral fornices around the cervix. It lies posterior to bladder and urethra and anterior to rectum and anal canal. The posterior fornix is related to the rectouterine pouch.

## Relationships and functional organization

Anterior relations: bladder and urethra. Posterior relations: rectouterine pouch superiorly, rectum, perineal body. Lateral fornix is related to ureter and uterine artery. Upper vagina drains to internal/external iliac nodes; lower vagina drains toward superficial inguinal nodes. Vaginal wall includes mucosa, muscular layer, and adventitia.

## Why is the perineal body relevant to the lower vaginal region?

The perineal body is a fibromuscular convergence between the vaginal outlet and anal region, linking several supporting muscles and fasciae. It contributes to the integrity of the perineal support system rather than acting as a separate organ. The upper posterior vaginal region instead relates to the rectouterine pouch, so superior and inferior relations differ substantially. A complete account should trace anterior, posterior and lateral relations along the tube's length, not apply the fornix relationships to the entire vaginal wall.
