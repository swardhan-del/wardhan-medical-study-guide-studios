## Pelvic Diaphragm and Levator Ani

The pelvic diaphragm is formed by levator ani and coccygeus muscles with their fasciae. It supports pelvic organs, maintains continence, resists increases in intra-abdominal pressure, and separates pelvic cavity from perineum.

## Relationships and functional organization

Levator ani includes puborectalis, pubococcygeus, and iliococcygeus. Coccygeus lies posteriorly on the sacrospinous ligament region. Anal hiatus transmits anal canal. Urogenital hiatus transmits urethra in male and urethra plus vagina in female. Innervation is mainly from nerve to levator ani and branches from sacral plexus/pudendal region.

## Why does supporting pelvic organs require more than a flat sheet of muscle?

The floor has openings for outlets and is shaped around them, so support depends on muscle tone, connective-tissue attachments and the perineal body. Pressure rises during coughing or lifting must be resisted without permanently closing those outlets. Puborectalis changes anorectal geometry, while other levator fibers support adjacent viscera. Injury or weakness can alter several relationships simultaneously. The perineal membrane lies below the levator complex in the urogenital region and should not be drawn as the entire pelvic diaphragm.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

Puborectalis is part of levator ani and helps maintain the anorectal angle. Relaxation during defecation allows that angle to change; it is not the same structure as the circular external anal sphincter. Levator ani also supports pelvic organs during pressure changes. Its direct sacral innervation, chiefly through the nerve to levator ani, should not be reduced to a statement that every pelvic-floor muscle is supplied only by the pudendal nerve.
