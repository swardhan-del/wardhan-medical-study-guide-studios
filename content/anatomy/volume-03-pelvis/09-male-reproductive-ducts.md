## Epididymis, Ductus Deferens, and Sperm Pathway

Sperm pass from seminiferous tubules to straight tubules, rete testis, efferent ductules, epididymal duct, ductus deferens, ejaculatory duct, and prostatic urethra. The epididymis is the maturation and storage organ; the ductus deferens is the thick-walled conducting duct.

Epididymis has head, body, tail. Ductus deferens begins at tail of epididymis, ascends in spermatic cord, passes through inguinal canal, crosses pelvic brim, and joins duct of seminal vesicle to form ejaculatory duct. Ampulla is the terminal dilated part of ductus deferens. Ejaculatory duct opens into prostatic urethra at seminal colliculus region. Named parts of the ductus deferens: testicular/scrotal part, funicular part in the spermatic cord, inguinal part in the inguinal canal, and pelvic part ending as the ampulla. Specimen rule: follow it from the tail of epididymis upward in the spermatic cord, through the inguinal canal, then posterior to the bladder toward the seminal vesicle.

## Seminal Vesicles and Ejaculatory Duct

Seminal vesicles are paired glands posterior to the bladder and anterior to the rectum. Each duct joins the ductus deferens to form the ejaculatory duct, which passes through prostate and opens into the prostatic urethra.

Seminal vesicles lie superior to prostate and lateral to ampulla of ductus deferens. They are posterior-inferior to bladder and anterior to rectum. Upper parts may be peritoneum-related. Ejaculatory duct forms by union of duct of seminal vesicle and ductus deferens. Ejaculatory ducts open on the seminal colliculus region of prostatic urethra.

## Prostate

The prostate is a fibromuscular gland inferior to the bladder, surrounding the prostatic urethra. It lies posterior to pubic symphysis, anterior to rectum, and superior to the pelvic diaphragm. It receives ejaculatory ducts and drains prostatic ducts into the prostatic urethra.

The prostate base is superior at the bladder neck; its apex is inferior near the external urethral sphincter and perineal membrane region. Anterior relation: pubic symphysis/retropubic space. Posterior relation: rectum, allowing digital rectal examination. Prostatic urethra and ejaculatory ducts pass through it. Venous drainage to prostatic venous plexus, communicating with vertebral venous plexus.

## Why does a posterior bladder diagram help locate the seminal vesicles?

Seminal vesicles lie behind the bladder and above the prostate, lateral to the terminal deferent ampullae. Their ducts join the deferent ducts and pass through prostate as ejaculatory ducts. The rectum lies posteriorly, while the distal ureters approach the bladder near these reproductive routes. A posterior view therefore reveals relationships that are obscured on an anterior bladder outline. It also makes clear that urinary and reproductive pathways remain separate until the reproductive ducts enter the urethral system.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

Sperm travels from seminiferous tubules through straight tubules, rete testis, efferent ductules, epididymis and ductus deferens. The duct of the seminal vesicle joins the deferent duct to form an ejaculatory duct through the prostate. The seminal vesicle contributes secretion; it is not a storage sac through which sperm must pass. The prostate surrounds the proximal urethral route, while its posterior surface faces the rectum, explaining a clinically useful relationship.
