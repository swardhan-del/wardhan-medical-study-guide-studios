## Ureter

The ureter is a muscular retroperitoneal tube conducting urine from renal pelvis to urinary bladder. It descends on the psoas major, crosses the pelvic brim near iliac vessels, then enters the bladder obliquely to form an anti-reflux mechanism.

## Relationships and functional organization

Three classic constrictions: ureteropelvic junction, crossing of pelvic brim/iliac vessels, and intramural passage through bladder wall. Blood supply is segmental: renal, gonadal, common/internal iliac, superior/inferior vesical, uterine/vaginal or ductus deferens arteries. In female pelvis, ureter passes under uterine artery near cervix: water under the bridge. Autonomic pain referral follows T11-L2 region and can radiate toward groin. Separate ureteric constrictions from ureteric crossings. Constrictions are stone-lodging points; crossings are topographic relationships the examiner may ask on a specimen. Main crossings to recite: the ureter descends anterior to psoas major/genitofemoral nerve region, is crossed anteriorly by gonadal vessels, crosses the iliac vessels at the pelvic brim, and then passes under ductus deferens in male or under uterine artery in female. Sex-specific trap: in male, ductus deferens crosses over ureter; in female, uterine artery crosses over ureter near cervix/parametrium.

## Why does ureteral blood supply need to be described as segmental?

Branches reach the ureter from nearby renal, gonadal, iliac and pelvic arterial sources along its course. These create longitudinal communications in surrounding tissue rather than one single artery running from kidney to bladder. The regional sources explain why abdominal and pelvic segments have different vascular neighbors. Peristaltic urine transport occurs in the muscular ureteral wall, while the intramural course at the bladder supports resistance to reflux. Blood supply, propulsion and anti-reflux geometry are distinct parts of its functional anatomy.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

It descends retroperitoneally, crosses the pelvic brim near iliac vessels and passes beneath the uterine artery in the female pelvis or beneath the ductus deferens in the male pelvis. Its oblique intramural course helps resist reflux as bladder pressure rises. A crossing landmark and a functional narrowing are related concepts but are not identical claims about every ureter.
