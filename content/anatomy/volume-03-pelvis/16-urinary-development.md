## Intermediate Mesoderm and Urogenital Ridge

The urogenital system develops mainly from intermediate mesoderm, which forms the urogenital ridge. This ridge gives rise to nephrogenic cord for urinary organs and gonadal ridge for reproductive organs.

Intermediate mesoderm lies between paraxial and lateral plate mesoderm. Nephrogenic cord forms pronephros, mesonephros, and metanephros in sequence. Gonadal ridge receives primordial germ cells. Cloaca and urogenital sinus contribute to lower urinary and genital outlet structures. Mesonephric and paramesonephric ducts are central to genital tract development.

## Kidney Development

Permanent kidneys develop from interaction between ureteric bud and metanephric mesenchyme. The ureteric bud forms ureter, renal pelvis, calyces, and collecting ducts; metanephric mesenchyme forms nephrons from Bowman capsule to distal convoluted tubule.

Pronephros is transient and nonfunctional. Mesonephros is temporary and contributes duct remnants. Metanephros is the permanent kidney. Ureteric bud branches to form collecting system. Metanephric blastema differentiates into nephron components. Kidneys ascend from pelvis to abdomen and receive changing blood supply from aorta.

## Bladder and Urethra Development

The urinary bladder develops mainly from the vesical part of the urogenital sinus. The trigone is related to incorporation of mesonephric ducts, but its epithelium becomes endodermal. The allantois becomes the urachus, later the median umbilical ligament.

Cloaca is divided by urorectal septum into urogenital sinus anteriorly and anorectal canal posteriorly. Vesical part of urogenital sinus forms most of bladder. Pelvic part contributes urethra. Allantois remnant becomes urachus and then median umbilical ligament. Most urethral epithelium is associated with urogenital sinus endoderm. Traditional accounts of distal glanular contribution vary; a simple ectoderm-only claim is not used here.

## What is reciprocal induction in permanent kidney formation?

The ureteric bud and metanephric mesenchyme signal to each other: branching of the collecting system and differentiation of nephron elements depend on their interaction. One structure does not simply grow into a fully formed kidney. Nephrons must connect to collecting ducts to create a continuous urinary route. During positional ascent, successive arterial supplies develop and some may persist as accessory vessels. Development therefore explains both the different epithelial components and common adult vascular variations.
