## Perineal Region and Triangles

The perineum is a diamond-shaped region bounded by pubic symphysis anteriorly, coccyx posteriorly, and ischial tuberosities laterally. A line between ischial tuberosities divides it into anterior urogenital triangle and posterior anal triangle.

Urogenital triangle contains external genitalia and urethral structures. Anal triangle contains anal canal, external anal sphincter, and ischioanal fossae. Perineal body is a central fibromuscular node between urogenital and anal triangles. Perineal membrane closes much of the urogenital triangle deep to superficial pouch. Pelvic diaphragm forms roof of the perineum.

## Superficial Perineal Pouch

The superficial perineal pouch lies between perineal membrane and superficial perineal fascia. In males it contains root of penis and associated muscles; in females it contains erectile tissues, greater vestibular glands, and corresponding muscles.

Male contents: bulb and crura of penis, proximal spongy urethra region, ischiocavernosus, bulbospongiosus, superficial transverse perineal muscles, branches of internal pudendal vessels/nerves. Female contents: clitoris crura, bulbs of vestibule, greater vestibular glands, ischiocavernosus, bulbospongiosus, superficial transverse perineal muscles. Perineal membrane forms deep boundary. Superficial perineal fascia and Colles fascia are important for spread of fluid.

## Deep Perineal Pouch and Urogenital Diaphragm

The deep perineal pouch lies superior to the perineal membrane and contains sphincteric urethral muscle complex and deep transverse perineal structures. In males, it also contains bulbourethral glands and membranous urethra.

Male deep pouch: membranous urethra, external urethral sphincter, deep transverse perineal muscles, bulbourethral glands, pudendal branches. Female deep pouch: urethra, external urethral sphincter complex, compressor urethrae/urethrovaginal sphincter region, deep transverse perineal structures, vessels/nerves. Perineal membrane is the inferior boundary of the deep pouch. This region is essential for continence and urethral support.

## How can fascial attachments influence the spread of fluid in the superficial perineal region?

The superficial pouch lies between the perineal membrane and membranous superficial fascia. Attachments of that fascia to the perineal margins constrain some routes while continuity with superficial abdominal fascia permits others. The resulting pattern depends on which tissue layer contains the fluid and where a breach occurs. This is why drawing only muscles without fascia gives an incomplete explanation. The deep perineal region has different boundaries and should not be assumed to communicate with every superficial compartment in the same way.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The perineum lies below the pelvic diaphragm. A line between the ischial tuberosities divides urogenital and anal triangles; these are regional subdivisions, not layers. The perineal membrane is a key boundary in the urogenital triangle. Traditional descriptions of a closed urogenital diaphragm can obscure the actual sphincter and connective-tissue arrangement, especially in females. Name the membrane, sphincter complex and pelvic diaphragm separately rather than treating them as interchangeable structures.
