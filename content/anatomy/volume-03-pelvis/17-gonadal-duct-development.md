## Gonad Formation and Primordial Germ Cells

Gonads develop from gonadal ridges formed by intermediate mesoderm, with primordial germ cells migrating from yolk sac region to the ridge. The indifferent gonad can become testis or ovary depending on genetic and hormonal pathways.

Primordial germ cells migrate along dorsal mesentery to gonadal ridge. SRY/testis-determining pathways promote testis development. Sertoli cells produce anti-Mullerian hormone. Leydig cells produce testosterone. Without AMH-mediated regression, paramesonephric ducts persist; ovarian and female tract differentiation also involve active genetic pathways.

## Male Genital Development

Male internal genital ducts develop mainly from mesonephric ducts under testosterone influence, while paramesonephric ducts regress under anti-Mullerian hormone. External genitalia masculinize under dihydrotestosterone.

Mesonephric duct derivatives: epididymis, ductus deferens, seminal vesicle, ejaculatory duct. Mesonephric tubules contribute efferent ductules. AMH causes paramesonephric duct regression. Testosterone supports mesonephric duct development. DHT supports prostate and external genital masculinization. Testis descent is guided by gubernaculum and processus vaginalis.

## Female Genital Development

Female internal genital organs develop mainly from paramesonephric ducts in the absence of AMH. The uterine tubes derive from unfused cranial parts; uterus, cervix, and upper vagina derive from fused caudal parts forming the uterovaginal canal.

Paramesonephric ducts form uterine tubes, uterus, cervix, and upper vagina. Lower vagina develops from urogenital sinus via sinovaginal bulbs/vaginal plate. Mesonephric ducts mostly regress, leaving remnants such as epoophoron and paroophoron. Ovaries descend into pelvis; gubernaculum contributes ovarian ligament and round ligament of uterus. Fusion defects can produce uterine anomalies.

## Why should AMH and testosterone be placed on separate arrows in a developmental map?

They arise from different testicular cell populations and act on different targets. Sertoli-derived AMH promotes paramesonephric regression, while Leydig-derived testosterone supports mesonephric differentiation. Conversion to DHT contributes to external genital and prostatic development. A map with one arrow labeled male hormone hides these distinctions and cannot explain selective changes in one pathway. The same principle applies to separating gonadal differentiation from duct development and external genital patterning: these processes interact but are not a single anatomical event.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

Sertoli-cell anti-Mullerian hormone promotes paramesonephric regression, while Leydig-cell testosterone supports mesonephric duct differentiation. Dihydrotestosterone contributes to external genital and prostatic development. These are distinct signaling roles, so a hormone name must be paired with its target tissue. Female reproductive development is also an active genetic process; it should not be described merely as doing nothing. Classical duct maps are useful but simplify the developmental contributions to the vagina.
