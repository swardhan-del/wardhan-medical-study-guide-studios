## Internal Iliac Artery

The internal iliac artery is the main arterial supply of the pelvis, gluteal region, perineum, and many pelvic organs. For oral exams, organize branches into parietal and visceral branches.

Parietal branches supply pelvic wall, gluteal region, and perineum. Visceral branches supply pelvic organs. Important parietal branches: iliolumbar, lateral sacral, obturator, superior gluteal, inferior gluteal, internal pudendal. Important visceral branches: umbilical/superior vesical, inferior vesical in male, uterine and vaginal in female, middle rectal. Internal pudendal artery exits and re-enters via sciatic foramina to supply perineum and external genitalia.

## Pelvic Lymphatics

Pelvic lymph drainage follows pelvic vessels. External iliac nodes drain anterior pelvic and lower abdominal structures; internal iliac nodes drain most pelvic viscera and deep perineum; sacral nodes drain posterior pelvic structures; common iliac nodes receive from external, internal, and sacral nodes.

External iliac nodes receive lymph from lower limb pathways, anterior abdominal wall, bladder, uterus/vagina regions, and anterior pelvic organs. Internal iliac nodes receive from pelvic viscera, deep perineum, gluteal region, and thigh region. Sacral nodes receive from posterior pelvic wall, rectum, bladder, prostate, and cervix. Common iliac nodes are the collecting station before lumbar nodes.

## Why is gonadal drainage an exception to a simple pelvic-node rule?

Ovaries and testes develop on the posterior abdominal wall and retain lymphatic connections toward lumbar nodes. Other pelvic organs drain through internal, external or sacral iliac routes according to region and connections. The uterine fundus can also use ovarian-associated and round-ligament pathways, illustrating overlapping routes. Therefore present-day organ location alone cannot predict the first node group. Tissue origin and vascular companions provide additional clues, but a complete map should acknowledge communications rather than assigning one exclusive node to every organ.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

Internal iliac branching is variable, so start with territories and then learn the usual named branches. Internal pudendal branches accompany the pudendal nerve into the perineum; superior gluteal branches pass above piriformis. Lymphatic drainage is not a simple copy of arterial supply: organs may have multiple routes, and gonads retain lumbar drainage related to development. Follow a node chain toward common iliac and lumbar nodes rather than treating each node group as an isolated endpoint.
