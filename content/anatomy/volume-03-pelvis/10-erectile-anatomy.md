## Penis and Mechanism of Erection

The penis contains paired corpora cavernosa dorsally and one corpus spongiosum ventrally surrounding the spongy urethra. Erection depends on parasympathetic vasodilation through cavernous nerves, arterial inflow, and venous compression within erectile tissue.

The penile root includes a single bulb and paired crura attached in the perineum. Body contains corpora cavernosa and corpus spongiosum. Glans is expansion of corpus spongiosum. Deep arteries of penis supply corpora cavernosa; dorsal artery supplies glans/skin region. Parasympathetic pathways support erection; sympathetic pathways contribute to emission, and somatic pudendal pathways contribute to ejaculatory expulsion. The suspensory ligament of the penis attaches the root/body region to the pubic symphysis and stabilizes the penis during erection. Specimen orientation: paired crura belong to the corpora cavernosa; the bulb belongs to the corpus spongiosum and continues into the glans.

## External Female Genital Organs

External female genital organs include mons pubis, labia majora, labia minora, clitoris, vestibule, external urethral opening, vaginal opening, bulbs of vestibule, and greater vestibular glands. They are mainly supplied by internal pudendal vessels and pudendal nerve branches.

Clitoris contains erectile tissue homologous to corpora cavernosa. Bulbs of vestibule are erectile tissue homologous to bulb of penis/corpus spongiosum region. Greater vestibular glands open into vestibule posterolateral to vaginal opening. Perineal nerve and dorsal nerve of clitoris are branches of pudendal nerve. Lymph from external genitalia largely drains to superficial inguinal nodes.

## Why is the urethra associated with corpus spongiosum rather than enclosed between the cavernosa?

Corpus spongiosum surrounds the spongy urethral route and expands into bulb and glans, while paired cavernosa provide the principal rigid erectile bodies. Their connective-tissue and vascular arrangements differ, allowing the urethral channel to remain a distinct structure during erectile changes. The crura are cavernous attachments, whereas the bulb belongs to spongiosum. Labeling all erectile tissue identically would obscure the urethral course and the correspondence between root, body and distal expansions.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The paired corpora cavernosa continue proximally as crura; the corpus spongiosum expands as the bulb proximally and glans distally. Vascular filling depends strongly on parasympathetic and local signaling. Emission involves sympathetic pathways, while expulsion also needs somatic rhythmic muscle activity through pudendal pathways. Thus ejaculation cannot be explained as a purely sympathetic event. Homologous erectile structures exist in the clitoris and vestibular bulbs, but the female urethra does not run through the clitoris.
