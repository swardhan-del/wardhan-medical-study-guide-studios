## Uterus: Parts, Position, and Supports

The uterus is a thick-walled muscular organ between bladder and rectum. It has fundus, body, isthmus, cervix, and uterine cavity. Normal position is anteversion relative to vagina and anteflexion between body and cervix.

Anteversion: cervix/uterus axis inclines forward relative to vagina. Anteflexion: body bends forward relative to cervix. Anterior relation: bladder and vesicouterine pouch. Posterior relation: rectouterine pouch, rectum, and small intestine. Supports include pelvic diaphragm, perineal body, transverse cervical/cardinal ligaments, uterosacral ligaments, pubocervical fascia, round ligament, broad ligament, and vagina. Layers from inside outward: endometrium, myometrium, and perimetrium/serosa. Specimen orientation: anteriorly the uterus faces the bladder and the round ligament/broad ligament is often prominent; posteriorly it faces the rectum and rectouterine pouch, and the ovaries are often more visible. Use the organ order to orient a sagittal female pelvis: pubic symphysis, bladder, uterus/vagina, rectum, sacrum.

## Broad Ligament and Parametrium

The broad ligament is a double layer of peritoneum extending from the sides of the uterus to the lateral pelvic walls and floor. It is divided into mesometrium, mesosalpinx, and mesovarium. The parametrium is the connective tissue between layers near the cervix and lateral uterus.

Mesometrium is the largest part related to uterus. Mesosalpinx encloses the uterine tube. Mesovarium attaches to ovary. Contents include uterine tube, round ligament of uterus, ovarian ligament, uterine vessels, nerves, lymphatics, and remnants such as epoophoron/paroophoron. Parametrium contains uterine vessels and is clinically important near ureter.

## Uterine Vessels, Lymphatics, and Relations

The uterine artery, a branch of the internal iliac artery, runs medially in the base of the broad ligament and crosses superior to the ureter near the cervix. Uterine venous drainage follows uterine venous plexuses to internal iliac veins. Lymph drainage varies by region.

Uterine artery crosses over ureter: water under the bridge. Fundus drains partly to para-aortic/lumbar and superficial inguinal nodes via round ligament route. Body drains mainly to external iliac nodes. Cervix drains to internal iliac and sacral nodes. Lateral fornix of vagina is related to ureter and uterine artery. The uterine artery reaches the uterus near the cervix/isthmus region and divides into ascending and descending branches. The ascending branch supplies the body/fundus and gives tubal branches that anastomose with ovarian artery branches near the uterine tube, especially around the ampulla. The descending branch supplies the cervix and vagina and continues with vaginal arterial branches.

## Why do version and flexion require different reference axes?

Version compares the cervical or uterine orientation with the vaginal axis; flexion compares the body with the cervix. A uterus can therefore change one angle without the same change in the other. The usual anteverted, anteflexed pattern is common but not obligatory. Bladder filling, support and individual anatomy influence position. A sagittal sketch should include vagina, cervix and body as separate segments, otherwise two different spatial relationships collapse into the vague statement that the uterus points forward.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

Anteversion compares the cervix with the vagina; anteflexion compares the uterine body with the cervix. Support comes from coordinated pelvic-floor, fascial and ligamentous structures. The broad ligament is mainly a peritoneal fold, not the principal load-bearing support. Cardinal and uterosacral support and the levator complex matter more mechanically. Uterine lymph can follow more than one route, including a fundal route toward superficial inguinal nodes along the round ligament.
