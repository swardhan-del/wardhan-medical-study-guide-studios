## Testis and Scrotum

The testis is a paired male gonad located in the scrotum. It produces spermatozoa in seminiferous tubules and testosterone from interstitial Leydig cells. It is covered by tunica albuginea and partially by tunica vaginalis, a remnant of processus vaginalis.

Testis has superior and inferior poles, medial/lateral surfaces, anterior/posterior borders. Epididymis lies along posterior aspect of testis. Tunica vaginalis has parietal and visceral layers around the anterior/lateral testis region. Arterial supply is testicular artery from abdominal aorta. Venous drainage is pampiniform plexus to testicular veins: right to IVC, left to left renal vein. Lymph drains to lumbar/para-aortic nodes, not superficial inguinal nodes. Developmental orientation: the testis develops high on the posterior abdominal wall near the kidney region, then descends through the inguinal canal into the scrotum. Thermoregulation has three practical mechanisms: dartos wrinkles scrotal skin, cremaster elevates the testis, and the pampiniform plexus cools testicular arterial blood by countercurrent heat exchange.

## Spermatic Cord Coverings and Contents

The spermatic cord extends from deep inguinal ring to testis and contains ductus deferens, testicular vessels, pampiniform plexus, autonomic nerves, lymphatics, genital branch of genitofemoral nerve, and remnants of processus vaginalis. Its coverings derive from layers of the abdominal wall.

External spermatic fascia derives from external oblique aponeurosis. Cremaster muscle/fascia derives from internal oblique. Internal spermatic fascia derives from transversalis fascia. Contents include ductus deferens, artery to ductus deferens, testicular artery, pampiniform plexus, lymphatics, autonomic plexus, genital branch of genitofemoral nerve, and processus vaginalis remnants. Add the cremasteric artery when giving a full contents answer. The artery to the ductus deferens usually arises from the vesical artery system of the internal iliac artery. Keep the examiner trap: ilioinguinal nerve passes through the inguinal canal but is not a true spermatic cord content.

## How do the pampiniform plexus and cremaster contribute to temperature regulation?

The pampiniform venous plexus surrounds incoming arterial channels and supports countercurrent heat exchange, reducing the temperature of arterial blood approaching the testis. Cremaster changes testicular position relative to the body, while dartos alters scrotal skin configuration. These mechanisms operate together and do not replace the need for vascular perfusion. The plexus eventually drains to testicular veins, with the right typically entering IVC and the left entering left renal vein. Venous drainage asymmetry is separate from the bilateral arterial origin from aorta.

## Apply the anatomy and resolve a common confusion

The testis develops high on the posterior abdominal wall and retains abdominal vessels and lymphatic drainage after descent. Scrotal skin has a different superficial inguinal drainage pattern. The cord carries ductus deferens, testicular vessels, pampiniform plexus and associated structures; the ilioinguinal nerve runs in the canal but is not enclosed as a true cord content. Preserve the distinction between testis, its coverings and the surrounding skin when answering vessel or lymph questions.
