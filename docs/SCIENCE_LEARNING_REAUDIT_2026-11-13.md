## What to borrow from other learning resources

- **OpenStax** separates location, structure and flow. We used that progression and linked directly to the relevant chapter at each diagram and question. [Heart anatomy](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/19-1-heart-anatomy) · [Lungs](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-2-the-lungs) · [Gas exchange](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/22-4-gas-exchange)
- **University of Michigan BlueLink** supplies specimen-oriented lab material. The heart unit asks learners to identify the apex and base on the external specimen before checking labels. [BlueLink Heart Lab](https://sites.google.com/a/umich.edu/bluelink/curricula/m1-foundational-anatomy/sequence-2-cardiology/heart/lablink)
- **Histology Guide** supplies a real H&E lung section with both bronchiole and alveolar views. The lung unit asks learners to compare a continuous airway wall with several air spaces separated by thin septa. [MH 138 Lung](https://www.histologyguide.org/slideview/MH-138-lung/17-slide-1.html?page=2&x=35347&y=15107&z=100)
- **PhET** uses focused controls and student think-aloud sessions. For Wardhan, that suggests a model with one visible decision and a follow-up task that shows whether students can explain the result. Start with the hide-and-reveal control and observe how students actually use it. [PhET research](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/research)
- **Retrieval practice research** supports testing recall after study. Use My Study to ask a fresh question on a later day and track it separately from immediate feedback. The published research does not measure this website. [Roediger and Karpicke](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/)

## Work order

1. **Make each first action clear.** Review #91 through three uncoached tasks, starting a new topic, looking up a confusing concept and finding a specific kind of practice. Make the explanation the prominent first action on the three pilot lessons where necessary. Give Biostatistics a discoverable entry and a consistent coverage label.
2. **Validate the heart and lung pilots.** Have a qualified reviewer check anatomical orientation, valve order, fissures, diffusion arrows and each answer. Ask learners to find the relevant view, explain a new case and return to a saved question. Repeat the application task after a delay using a new example. Use the repository’s [beginner protocol](PRELAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md) to record results.
3. **Build the next two anatomy units.** Start with spinal cord orientation and a separate sensory-to-motor pathway, then compare a joint from two orientations with a movement prediction. Use the current diagrams and lesson sources. Add a worked example, a fresh application and a targeted external specimen task for each unit.
4. **Extend the same teaching pattern across sciences.** For histology, compare preparation, scale and tissue clues. For physiology, let a student change one variable and predict its effect. For biochemistry, compare two graphs and ask for the mechanism. For statistics, ask the student to choose an interpretation from a small real or explicitly fictional dataset. Track prerequisites, source, review, illustration rights and coverage in the existing records.

The new teaching illustrations and questions are original. External resources remain links to their publishers. The audit measures software and editorial experience and does not establish student outcomes.
